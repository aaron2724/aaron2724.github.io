01 / The business question

What needs correcting before the new system can be trusted?

An enterprise HR and payroll transformation required data held in different applications to be prepared, compared and validated. The challenge was not simply transferring fields. It was understanding whether discrepancies reflected actual errors, legitimate differences in business rules or issues requiring a decision from HR or Payroll.

Can we explain material differences between source, migration output and target records before they affect employment or payroll information?

My contribution: I carried out hands-on profiling, cleansing, reconciliation, exception investigation and file preparation. I also supported testing and communicated findings to business and technology stakeholders as part of a wider delivery team.

02 / Data understanding

Multiple systems, different meanings

The analysis involved employee attributes such as identifiers, employment status, organisational assignments, work patterns, leave classifications and remuneration-related fields. Different applications can store comparable information using different formats, effective dates or permitted values.

Technical risk

Records fail to align

A record may exist in both files but differ because keys, formats or timing do not align.

Business risk

Exceptions misclassified

A harmless formatting difference can be mistaken for an error, while a meaningful discrepancy can be overlooked.

03 / Analytical workflow

From raw extracts to decisions

Excel and Power Query supported repeatable comparisons and investigation outputs. This is a simplified representation of the approach, not a proprietary system design.

01 · Input

Profile

Review keys, field types, missing values and structures.

02 · Prepare

Standardise comparable values and apply approved mappings.

03 · Analyse

Compare

Flag discrepancies and categorise the affected fields.

04 · Resolve

Retest

Investigate with owners and check corrected outputs.

Why it mattered: The process helped distinguish widespread mapping patterns from individual employee-level exceptions, making findings more useful for stakeholder review.

05 / Logic and quality control

Making checks repeatable

  • Reliable keys: align records using appropriate identifiers and known formatting rules.
  • Field-level comparisons: identify exactly which attribute differs.
  • Business-rule review: separate expected mappings from genuine errors.
  • Exception grouping: summarise related issues for the correct decision-maker.
  • Retesting: verify what changed following an agreed correction.
Illustrative logic, not production code
IF records cannot be matched reliably:
    Flag = "Key review"
ELSE IF approved source-to-target mapping agrees:
    Flag = "Match"
ELSE IF a documented rule explains the difference:
    Flag = "Confirm accepted mapping"
ELSE:
    Flag = "Investigate with field owner"
06 / Tangible contribution

What I produced and supported

  • Reconciliation workbooks: repeatable Excel and Power Query comparisons highlighting source-to-target discrepancies.
  • Exception summaries: grouped mismatch reporting that supported investigation and prioritisation.
  • Migration preparation: cleaned and mapped data checked against target requirements before upload.
  • Testing support: investigation of load exceptions and post-transition differences alongside relevant stakeholders.

These descriptions intentionally exclude internal file layouts, operational configuration and employee-level information.

07 / Outcomes and evidence

The improvement was in the quality of decisions

The analytical contribution made fragmented data more comparable and issues more traceable, helping stakeholders evaluate discrepancies and support migration validation and remediation.

Evidence boundary: This case study does not claim a measured reduction in defects, time savings, financial benefits or error-free payroll outcomes. Such results would require verified, approved evidence.

What I learned

A mismatch flag is only a starting point. Effective data migration analysis requires understanding the business meaning of each difference, identifying the appropriate owner and turning technical findings into practical next steps.
