Project context As part of a wider HR and payroll transformation, the organisation needed to prepare and validate employee and workforce information across multiple systems. The work involved legacy and target HR and payroll environments including Preceda, iChris, iHub and Kronos, with dependencies on wider workforce management structures. At a scale of more than 5,000 employees across Australia and New Zealand, differences in system fields, business rules and existing records made reliable data migration a significant workstream. Public case study note. This page describes my contribution and working approach at a high level. Employee data, internal file layouts, configurations and unpublished business results are intentionally excluded. 02. The challenge Data that is suitable for one system is not necessarily ready for another. Employee identifiers, names, employment attributes, remuneration and working arrangements needed to be aligned with target requirements, while source-to-target discrepancies had to be distinguished from legitimate business differences. The challenge was therefore not simply transferring records. It was creating a repeatable way to identify, explain and resolve exceptions so that HR, Payroll and Technology stakeholders could make informed decisions before and after migration activities. 03. My role and contribution Working as a Business Analyst, I carried out hands-on analysis and data remediation, coordinated clarification of business rules with stakeholders and helped translate technical exceptions into actionable validation work. Profiled and compared employee and payroll-related datasets across legacy and target systems. Investigated mismatched fields, missing values and inconsistent employee or position attributes. Prepared and checked data for structured uploads into the target HR environment. Worked with HR, Payroll and Technology teams to confirm mapping rules and resolve exceptions. Supported migration testing, user acceptance testing, cutover readiness and post-load reconciliation. Summarised analysis and outstanding issues for project stakeholders. 04. Analytical approach I approached reconciliation as a structured sequence, moving from raw extracts to validated outputs and clearly categorised exceptions. Step 01 Profile source data Understand available fields, data types, missing values and inconsistent formats before attempting comparisons. Step 02 Transform and align Use Excel and Power Query to standardise comparison fields, apply mapping logic and prepare comparable records. Step 03 Reconcile and investigate Identify mismatches and exceptions, distinguish formatting differences from substantive issues, and trace causes. Step 04 Validate and report Confirm resolutions with business owners, prepare corrected outputs and communicate outstanding migration risks. The work covered data such as personal and employment fields, employee and position identifiers, job and cost centre information, remuneration groups, work patterns, leave groups and salary-related attributes. 05. Deliverables Repeatable data reconciliation: structured comparisons and validation checks using Excel and Power Query. Exception analysis: mismatch reporting and investigation outputs to make remediation work easier to prioritise. Migration data preparation: cleansed and validated information for target-system upload and review. Testing and readiness support: analysis of load discrepancies, validation findings and cutover dependencies. Stakeholder reporting: clear summaries of data quality issues, remediation progress and decisions requiring input. Any future visual examples will use synthetic or anonymised data rather than internal records. 06. Outcomes and impact Helped identify and address source-to-target discrepancies ahead of migration milestones. Improved traceability of data quality issues through structured exception identification and follow-up. Provided stakeholders with clearer evidence for confirming business rules and validating migration data. Contributed to testing and migration readiness through reconciliation of target data and investigation of load issues. Capability demonstrated: combining hands-on data analytics with business analysis and stakeholder collaboration to reduce uncertainty in complex systems transformation. Specific time savings, error-reduction percentages and financial impact are intentionally not claimed without verified supporting evidence. 07. What this project demonstrates Reliable migration outcomes depend on more than technical transformations. They require careful interpretation of business rules, repeatable validation, clear ownership of exceptions and communication between operational and technology teams.
